# PocketToons synthetic dataset

This deterministic SQLite dataset supports product-analytics investigation and evaluation. Run with Python 3, no third-party packages:

```sh
python3 examples/pockettoons/generate.py --output pockettoons.sqlite
python3 examples/pockettoons/check_data.py pockettoons.sqlite
# Optional: add the data layers built so far (run on a copy first).
python3 examples/pockettoons/build_layers.py pockettoons.sqlite
```

The generator refuses to overwrite an existing database. The main random seed is 8126; marketing uses a separate deterministic stream seeded with 10844 so marketing variation does not change other generated records. The six analysis months are April–September 2026. There are 10,400 existing March signups; the monthly signup cohorts from April through September are 1,600, 2,201, 2,653, 2,477, 2,949, and 1,720. All times are UTC. The database has 24,000 users, 247,715 sessions, 325,149 views, and 6,618 payment/refund attempts (including 317 failed attempts: 30 PPV checkouts and 287 first renewal attempts that a later retry recovers); the file is about 0.66 GB. Activity variation uses its own deterministic stream (seed 8126 + 4409), so it does not change monthly MAU or revenue. Its 12 titles have between 20 and 39 episodes each.

## Synthetic story and metric definitions

This section contains expected synthetic findings. Exclude it from the agent's investigation context when evaluating independent discovery.

Users are active on a varying number of days each month; monthly stickiness (average DAU ÷ MAU) is about 12%, and about 60% of monthly active users watch at least 60 seconds (some months a non-paying user only browses). Every signup happens in a visit at the signup time; signup times follow the same local daily pattern. Countries are uneven: about 30% US, 19% Brazil, 16% Mexico, 15% Philippines, 12% Indonesia and 8% Thailand. Session starts follow each country's local daily pattern with an evening peak, platforms are uneven (Android about 45% of users' primary devices, iOS 35%, web 20%) and some users use a second installation on another platform, about 12% of views end within the first minute, and about a quarter of views watch the episode to the end, sometimes followed by the next episode.

Quarter MAU is the average of monthly distinct active users, where activity is a session. Monthly MAU is 7,937, 9,134, 9,976, 10,138, 10,871, and 11,447, an approximately 20% increase in quarter-average MAU. Revenue is succeeded subscription and PPV charges less succeeded refunds, before fees/tax, measured at success time. All charges and refunds are stored as positive amounts; subtract refunds explicitly with `CASE WHEN type='refund' THEN -amount_usd ELSE amount_usd END`. Monthly net revenue is $9,863.83, $10,293.40, $9,987.77, $10,171.53, $9,819.93, and $10,175.51 respectively. The monthly values are controlled synthetic targets.

Daily marketing spend, impressions and clicks vary by channel and day; paid-social spend is higher from July onward. These are controlled synthetic observations, not proof of marketing causality. The later growth has an increased paid-social share; newer signup cohorts have lower 30-day subscription uptake than the immediately preceding cohorts. One hundred subscriptions leave each month, while replacements vary with the monthly target. Comparing unequal cohort ages is not a fair conversion test: use a fixed observation window and include only cohorts with a complete window. The checks compare May–June (88 of 4,854, 1.81%) with July–August (26 of 5,426, 0.48%). The March cohort is excluded from 30-day conversion because its observation window begins before data coverage starts in April. Channel comparisons must account for unknown sources; these patterns are associations, not experimental proof of marketing causality. The validation checks whether paid-social activity growth survives assigning all unknown Q1 users to paid social and none in Q2.

An arithmetic revenue bridge compares users who joined before July with users who joined in July or later. Q1 gross subscription charges were $29,970 from pre-July users; in Q2 they were $29,210.76 from those users and $769.23 from new users, before subtracting refunds. This cohort bridge demonstrates offsetting amounts, but does not by itself prove that all lost pre-July revenue was caused by cancellation; use subscription history to analyze the cancellations. PPV and refunds vary by month, including one additional purchase after a relisting.

Refunds comprise seven PPV refunds (about 3% of PPV purchases) and five subscription refunds, each linked to its original purchase. The fixture sets each PPV refund allocation's created_at and updated_at to its refund transaction time and models no allocation corrections. These UTC values are synthetic assumptions; they do not preserve prior versions. `refund_reason` is populated only on refunds and records a synthetic reported reason (`did_not_like_content`, `accidental_purchase`, or `poor_playback_quality`), not an independently verified cause. Subscription refunds close access when approved; prior views remain in the data.

## Data quality

The fixture marks `dataset_metadata.session_activity_coverage` as `complete_synthetic_v1` and includes synthetic `session_engagement` and view-linked `playback_heartbeat` records. The observations support session-gap and playback continuity tests. They are simulated timing evidence, not separate user interactions, views or watch-time increments. Completeness is specific to this synthetic fixture and must not be assumed for a real source. Run `python3 examples/pockettoons/test_sessions.py` for isolated session regression scenarios.

For this USD fixture, the checker validates two-decimal amounts and converts each value to integer cents with Python Decimal before its revenue, refund-cap and allocation checks. Invalid or excess-precision values require review. SQLite monetary columns remain REAL, and the checker-only conversion function is not stored in the database. FX and multi-currency minor-unit handling are not defined here. Run `python3 examples/pockettoons/test_transactions.py` for isolated payment-status, refund and exact-cent regression scenarios.

Episode lengths vary across the catalog and are constant across time in this fixture. `playback_segments` records continuous watched position ranges, including one rewind and one forward skip. Segment watch time reconciles to its view; per-attempt completion is based on merged unique ranges, not raw watch seconds. The fixture declares complete synthetic segment coverage; that is not a claim about other data sources. `content_tier_history` records effective-dated episode tiers, including a premium-to-free transition with viewing on both sides. `content_catalog_snapshot_at` defines the episode-tier snapshot. Actual historical purchase amounts remain in transactions; historical list-price and duration versions are not modeled. Run `python3 examples/pockettoons/test_content_episodes.py` and `python3 examples/pockettoons/test_playback_segments.py` for isolated episode, tier and segment regression scenarios.

Availability is recorded as continuous effective-dated status periods from the first tracked instant. The delist/relist example closes each PPV entitlement active at delisting and records notices for all four affected holders; later access requires a new entitlement. Already-ended or refunded entitlements are not rewritten. Run `python3 examples/pockettoons/test_availability.py` for isolated transition, missing-history, multi-holder, refund and relisting scenarios.

`users.acquisition_source` is `unknown` for 238 users. `raw_payment_source.raw_amount` mixes numeric and numeric-string values; six records are non-numeric and seven values are 100 times the dollar amount because the incoming value was cents labelled USD. `payment_quality_review` records evidence and corrections; `transactions.amount_usd` is the corrected analytical amount. Do not silently cast arbitrary text or assume every large value is wrong. The raw amount is not a second payment. The review table is explicitly available to analysts; using it is not the same as independently detecting the issue.

## Dataset coverage and boundaries

The dataset includes free, subscription and PPV playback, same-day resumes, multi-episode sessions, 112,982 browsing-only sessions (including logged-out visits on devices that never sign in, about 8% of sessions), pre-login browsing, checkouts without a recorded paywall link (about 5%) and transactions without a recorded checkout link (about 3%), checkout before successful purchases, clearly labelled subscription and PPV paywalls with non-purchaser observations, 317 failed payment attempts, eight-episode daily free limits reached about 130 times a month (each followed by a blocked ninth attempt), varied cancellation and refund-request times, subscription and PPV refunds, and one delist/relist/repurchase journey with four prior notices. Subscription paywalls include 4,800 non-purchasing sessions, 1,590 of which start checkout without purchase. PPV paywalls without a purchase occur in every month. Each initial subscription has an in-app paywall → checkout → purchase → premium-view journey; renewals remain off-session. Expired and refunded subscription states have explicit effective periods. Requests link to the original charge; refunds also link to that charge. All 183 analysis days have sessions. Subscription renewals follow the original day-of-month; PPV and refund times vary. Documented columns in the model and source-quality tables are present and required values are checked. The fixture covers selected statuses, events and invariants; monthly counts are controlled and the checks do not establish coverage of every model rule. Outliers live in a raw source table and do not distort curated transaction totals. It does not include the agent or the 15+ evaluation questions; those are separate later work.


Subscription history carries subscription_status_updated_at across consecutive intervals with the same status, including renewals. A status transition sets it to the effective time of that transition; it is not an ingestion timestamp or a full correction history.

For synthetic checkout journeys, the generator explicitly records triggering_paywall_event_id from the paywall and checkout pair constructed together. A NULL link means direct checkout or an uncaptured link. This relationship is not a payment link or causal proof, and it should not be inferred from nearest event time in other data.

For synthetic purchase journeys, the generator explicitly sets transactions.checkout_event_id for linked in-app payment attempts. Multiple attempts can point to one checkout; off-session or uncaptured links are NULL. The main fixture's failed payments are not a same-checkout retry sequence; that behavior is covered by isolated tests.
